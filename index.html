03 / Verified signals, unserious conclusions

The lore department.

The MOAI connection is real in Exyl's music catalogue. Any claim that Exyl knows this website, endorses it, or has joined the studio would be pure fiction.

FIELD NOTE / 2020🗿 → 🔊

MOAI entered the discography.

Exyl's official Bandcamp page describes the track as a collision of accumulated experimental ideas—and says Moai statues became a recurring theme in the artist's themes and artwork afterward.

FIELD NOTE / 2023💸 + 🗿

MOAI MONEY: financially carved.

The official video description says the piece began as a drift-phonk-flavoured section intended for a MOAI 2 collab, then became a separate track. The visual joke grew out of a real rough patch—not a made-up artist quote.

FIELD NOTE / 2023 → 2026🎛 + 🗿

The Kahoot to collab pipeline.

Exyl says the idea that became “Moai Samurai” started when Creo made a Kahoot-only mashup using chops from Exyl's older MOAI and Creo's “Dune.” Teminite later joined the collaboration; it was released in July 2026.

CONFIRMED SOURCEREAD THE STORY ↗
FIELD NOTE / CLYP → 2023HOW / 50:50

Paper Skies: from feedback server to collab.

In the official “HOW” notes, Exyl recalls looking up to Paper Skies on the old sharing site Clyp before they eventually made a bouncy, nostalgic complextro collaboration. The sample pack is a real companion release; this card's fake studio mythology is not.

FIELD NOTE / CREO × EXYL+1 BAR

Cosmic War: one bar forward.

Exyl's notes say a melody accidentally shifted forward by one bar during work on “Cosmic War”; adding vocal chops to the flute lead made the drop click. MOAI's official workflow is to call every happy accident a patented geological process.

The notification got a drop.

Exyl describes “Ping! 2” as the sequel to the Discord remix “Ping!”. A fan comment specifically praises its Discord soundfont. MOAI has submitted a request to turn the “message received” sound into a 64-bar anthem.
